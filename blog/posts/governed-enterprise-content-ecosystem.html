Content Strategy

From fragmented publishing to a governed content ecosystem

How I shaped a corporate-site strategy around reusable content, clear stewardship, connected platforms, omnichannel activation, and measurable digital insight.

By Sachin Kapoor30 August 20267 min read
Experience context
PepsiCo GCC
Transformation scope
Corporate sites · Content Hub · DAM · Analytics

A website redesign can improve pages while leaving the content operating model unchanged. Authors still recreate assets, teams still exchange files through disconnected services, approvals remain inconsistent, and performance insight arrives too late to influence the next decision.

At PepsiCo, I defined the corporate-site content strategy and led modernization from Sitefinity to Sitecore CMS across the corporate-site ecosystem. I established Sitecore Content Hub as the single source of truth, connected it with Sitecore and omnichannel publishing, helped retire fragmented content-sharing services, and introduced Bynder DAM with marketing and AI tools. I also integrated TryProFound GenAI analytics, FullStory, and GA4 to strengthen insight.

The transformation was not a CMS replacement. It was a move from site-by-site publishing to a governed content ecosystem.

Define the content problem before the platform

I begin with the work content must perform. Which audiences and journeys matter? Which messages need local expression and which require global consistency? What content should be reusable? Who has authority to create, approve, publish, update, and retire it? Which measures indicate that the content is useful?

These questions expose the operating model. They also prevent platform capability from becoming the strategy. A CMS, content hub, DAM, and analytics stack can enable better work, but only when the organization agrees on the content relationships and decisions those tools must support.

Create a governed source of truth

Sitecore Content Hub became the structured foundation for managing approved enterprise content. The key principle was not simply central storage. It was explicit lifecycle control: content types, metadata, ownership, workflow, versions, reuse, and retirement.

A source of truth becomes valuable when downstream channels can trust it. That requires a clear distinction between reusable content, presentation rules, market or channel variation, and digital assets. It also requires stewardship. Centralization without ownership creates a larger repository, not a more reliable ecosystem.

Give assets their own discipline

Bynder DAM addressed a related but distinct need: governed digital assets that can be discovered, rights-managed, reused, and activated across marketing and publishing experiences. Content and assets often move together, but their metadata, rights, renditions, and lifecycle controls are different.

I treated the connection between Content Hub, DAM, CMS, and channels as a product architecture. Interfaces, ownership, failure handling, and change management matter as much as the individual platform features.

Design for activation and learning

  • Model for reuseStructure content around meaning and purpose, not a single page layout.
  • Govern the lifecycleMake draft, review, approval, publication, localization, update, and retirement visible.
  • Separate content from channelLet channels present content appropriately while preserving a governed core.
  • Connect insight to decisionsUse behavioral, experience, and analytics signals to improve journeys and content priorities.
  • Operate the ecosystemMonitor integrations, publishing health, quality, adoption, and ownership as ongoing service responsibilities.

FullStory and GA4 strengthened behavioral and performance insight. TryProFound GenAI analytics added another way to explore digital signals. The important leadership move was to connect those insights back to content and platform decisions rather than allow analytics to remain a separate reporting activity.

Lead across business and technology boundaries

Enterprise content transformation crosses Corporate Communications, marketing, brand, legal, product, design, engineering, platform operations, analytics, security, and regional stakeholders. No single team can optimize the ecosystem alone.

My role was to create a shared direction, make platform and governance dependencies visible, and maintain a decision path from strategy through delivery and sustainment. That includes preparing operating teams early, defining acceptance, and ensuring the modernized experience can be supported after launch.

Content becomes an enterprise capability when it is governed once, activated many ways, measured continuously, and improved by accountable owners.

Closing perspective

The move from Sitefinity to Sitecore was an important technology change. The larger transformation was establishing an ecosystem in which content, assets, channels, governance, and insight could work together.

That is the content strategy I advocate: begin with audience and purpose, create a trusted foundation, design for reuse, assign stewardship, connect activation, and close the loop with evidence. The result is not just a better website. It is a more capable content organization.
